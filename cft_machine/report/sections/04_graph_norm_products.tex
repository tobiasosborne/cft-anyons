\section{A general criterion for limits of generator products}

The explicit coset construction eventually gives exact finite-core equality.
For other categorical models, exact stabilization may fail. VR-1 supplies
a quantitative replacement: jointly construct refinements and modes with
uniform graph bounds and summable defects. It is a conditional theorem, not
a result already verified for arbitrary anyon chains.

\subsection{Hypotheses and domain}

Let $\mathcal H_N$ be finite-dimensional, $J_N$ isometric, and let positive
auxiliary controls $K_N\ge1$ satisfy $K_{N+1}J_N=J_NK_N$. These controls
need not be physical Hamiltonians. Set
$\|v\|_{N,s}=\|K_N^sv\|$ for nonnegative integers $s$.

For all modes $n$ and required orders $s$, assume uniform bounds
\[
 \|T_{N,n}v\|_{N,s}\le C_{n,s}\|v\|_{N,s+1},
\]
adjoint pairing $T_{N,n}^*=T_{N,-n}$, and summable refinement errors
\[
 \|(T_{N+1,n}J_N-J_NT_{N,n})v\|_{N+1,s}
 \le e_{N,n,s}\|v\|_{N,s+1},\qquad \sum_Ne_{N,n,s}<\infty.
\]
Finally assume the weighted closure residuals tend to zero as maps between
the appropriate graph spaces. Both central constants from the lab book
remain explicit:
\[
 [T_{N,m},T_{N,n}]-(m-n)T_{N,m+n}
 -\frac{\delta_{m+n,0}}{12}(m^3c^*-mc)1=R_{N,mn}.
\]

\subsection{Proof mechanism VR-1}

\begin{description}
\item[$\langle1\rangle1$.] Complete the algebraic inductive union in
each graph norm. Exact $K$ intertwining makes the norms consistent.
Successive orthogonal finite-dimensional complements reduce $K$, so the
completions inject into one another and the stage projections $P_N$ are
contractive and converge strongly in each graph norm.
\item[$\langle1\rangle2$.] Telescope the one-step errors. On a vector
from stage $N$, the tail norm is at most
$d_{N,n,s}\|v\|_{N,s+1}$, where
$d_{N,n,s}=\sum_{j\ge N}e_{j,n,s}$. Completeness constructs a limiting
operator $T_n$; the uniform bound extends it between graph spaces.
\item[$\langle1\rangle3$.] For a word $w=(n_1,\ldots,n_r)$, replace
one factor at a time by its refined version. The one-step error constant is
\[
 E_{N,w,s}=\sum_{a=1}^r e_{N,n_a,s+a-1}
                  \prod_{j\ne a}C_{n_j,s+j-1}.
\]
It is summable. A direct fine-prefix/defect/coarse-suffix identity gives the
bound without assuming $J_NJ_N^*=1$.
\item[$\langle1\rangle4$.] Uniform bounds and strong convergence
between successive graph spaces identify the limiting word with the
product $T_{n_1}\cdots T_{n_r}$. The smooth domain given by the intersection
of all graph spaces is invariant.
\item[$\langle1\rangle5$.] Under the embedding, a finite identity
becomes $P_N$, not the identity on the completed space. Since $P_N\to1$,
the residual and product limits yield the stated bracket on the smooth
domain. Adjoint pairing passes by inner-product limits.
\item[$\langle1\rangle6$.] QED by $\langle1\rangle1$--$\langle1\rangle5$.
\end{description}

\subsection{What must still be proved for a new backend}

Finite observations of decreasing error do not prove the all-scale
majorants. A useful certificate would give, for example,
$e_{N,n,s}\le a_{n,s}\rho^N$ with a proved $\rho<1$; then its tail is
explicit. The theorem's exact auxiliary-control intertwining is stringent,
and no transfer to a different graph scale is implicit.

The theorem proves neither positive energy nor essential self-adjointness
of real smeared fields. It does not integrate the Lie algebra, construct
local observable algebras, or identify modular data. Moreover, arbitrary
Hilbert isometries do not automatically come from unital OAR observable maps.
The state and representation system must be the same one used by the
microscopic model, not a convenient comparison Hilbert space.
