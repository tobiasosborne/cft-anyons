## Ising A1/A2: detailed checks

1. OS `references/text/CFTFromLatticeFermions.txt:1460–1558` really defines
   unital injective CAR maps, Wilson-state pullbacks and the double-limit
   alpha criterion. A1's norm inequality follows from the same coherent
   isometric embeddings; it uses the global CAR norm before GNS and does
   not assume an arbitrary represented norm is faithful.

2. A2 makes the correct asymmetric continuity argument. The varying local
   observables A_M are only uniformly bounded and WOT-convergent, while
   the complementary sampled polynomial B_M converges **in norm** to a
   fixed b. Thus the commutator estimate
   `||[A_M,b]|| <= 2 sup_M||A_M|| ||B_M-b||`
   is valid. Multiplication by the fixed b is WOT-continuous. There is no
   multiplication of two independently varying weak limits.

3. The norm-approximating complementary polynomials exist by LL-1 and
   CAR norm continuity. Their supports are disjoint at every sufficiently
   fine lattice stage, even though their sharp continuum interpolants have
   sinc tails. Exact commutation follows at the finite stage and survives
   the CAR homomorphism. The proof does not incorrectly require disjoint
   support of those interpolants.

4. KL `references/cft-machine/realisation/KawahigashiLongo2002/source.tex:403–407`
   supplies the needed vacuum Haag duality. LMR
   `references/cft-machine/realisation/LongoMartinettiRehren2009/source/LMR.tex:1165–1168`
   identifies the even real-Fermi net with Vir_1/2. The fixed **global**
   even vacuum space H0 is the correct Hilbert space for applying that
   duality. For finitely many independent factors, the spatial tensor-product
   commutant is the tensor product of the commutants, giving product duality.

5. Both inclusions in the claimed maximal represented local algebra are
   present: every bounded local weak limit is in A(I), and sampled local
   polynomials themselves occur as uniformly bounded convergent sequences
   generating A(I). Taking the von Neumann algebra generated by the set of
   limits is essential; the set of weakly convergent sequences is not being
   asserted to be an algebra.

6. Removing the seam point does not lose L2-local fields. First cut a
   section off in a shrinking neighborhood of the point; then split its
   support into the two chart components. These cutoffs converge in L2,
   and their even field polynomials converge in CAR norm. A2 can therefore
   test the full complementary interval algebra even when the interval
   crosses the chosen seam. No density in a stronger stress-field Sobolev
   topology is needed for this field-algebra argument.

The obvious counterexample to a weaker proof is instructive: on an
orthonormal basis, |e0><en| and |en><e0| each tend weakly to zero, while their
product is the fixed projection |e0><e0|. This does not invalidate A2 because
its complementary approximant converges in norm, not merely weakly.

## Ising doubling

The displayed identity for r_M=M/2-s is exact:
sin(2pi*r_M/M)=sin(2pi*s/M). Hence these moving-edge excitations have
finite limiting energy. Distinct growing momentum labels are orthogonal
under the chosen sharp inclusions, so they have no norm-convergent
one-particle representative. Two distinct such excitations give an even
Fock vector and retain the same obstruction. Thus independent parity does
not remove the omitted-edge qualification. The note correctly preserves
this limitation instead of turning the chosen OAR limit into a theorem
about convergence of every low-energy state of the bare Hamiltonian.

## Smooth coset SC-1: decomposition and common core

1. The decomposition uses the **auxiliary** nonnegative free energy E,
   which is already established in CR-2. It does not assume the raw finite
   quartic coset Hamiltonian is positive. Finitely many fermion flavors,
   half-integer excitation energies and finite occupations give finite E
   multiplicity, so the increasing-grade construction is legitimate.

2. Previously generated algebraic descendant modules are invariant under
   C_n and C_-n. Adjoint pairing therefore makes their grade-by-grade
   orthogonal complement invariant. Its lowest remaining E block supplies
   highest-weight seeds. On a seed the exact norm identity
   `||C_-1 v||²=2h||v||²` proves h>=0; positivity has been derived from
   the actual ambient inner product rather than imposed by projection.

3. Descendant orthogonality follows by commuting raising modes onto the
   highest-weight seeds. A positive-grade singular descendant would be
   orthogonal to every descendant at its grade and hence to itself, so
   cannot survive in a positive Hilbert space. This justifies the
   irreducible unitary highest-weight quotient used in the source import.
   Finite E multiplicity also guarantees exhaustion, avoiding an unproved
   continuous direct-integral decomposition.

4. GKO `references/cft-machine/virasoro/GoddardKentOlive1986/source.txt:49–83`
   supplies uniqueness by (c,h) and the allowed unitary minimal weights.
   CW `references/cft-machine/coset-symmetry/CarpiWeiner2004/source.tex:393–445`
   and KL:1161–1178 support the corresponding projective positive-energy
   sector representation. CW:722–747 explicitly makes its mode-bound
   constant independent of the lowest weight. The direct-sum estimate
   consequently survives arbitrary spectator multiplicities.

5. CW:864–880 supplies essential self-adjointness on each module's
   finite-energy core. The algebraic sum of those cores is precisely D:
   a finite module sum has finite maximum starting free energy, and each
   finite E block involves only finitely many module contributions.
   Finite direct sums followed by component graph approximations prove
   that D is a core for the direct-sum self-adjoint stress.

The ambient representation need not have scalar exp(2pi*i*C0). Applying
CW directly to that full representation would have been invalid. The
modulewise application followed by a direct sum is the required repair,
and it is present. Only the vacuum module is identified as the single
projective vacuum Diff representation.

## Smooth coset SC-2: cutoff and functional-calculus audit

The simultaneous threshold is conservative and correct. For retained
|n|<=K_M, the CR-2 condition follows from
`R_M-K_M >= M/16-1 > e`; its second condition follows from
`M/2-1/2-e-2R_M >= M/4-1/2-e > 0`.
The stated M>=32(e+1) satisfies both inequalities. Therefore the retained
microscopic sum equals the continuum partial sum on every fixed P_e,
including vectors outside the coset vacuum module.

The remaining tail is a continuum weighted Fourier tail and is controlled
by the modulewise c-dependent bound. No uncontrolled finite-lattice tail
or interchange of infinite cutoff limits occurs. The actual lift is a
unital CAR representation, not a projection onto V_b or the vacuum module.
Self-adjointness of each finite real-smearing matrix follows from the
paired mode adjoints, even when the raw finite Hamiltonian has negative
energy on other states.

The resolvent identity in SC-2 <1>3 is correct. Its right side tends to
zero on D and the resolvent norm is at most one; the density of (T-i)D
extends convergence to all vectors. Applying the self-adjoint functional
calculus then yields compact-time strong unitary convergence. Finite
products follow from a telescoping expansion with norm-one factors.
This argument supplies no finite-scale projective Diff group law and
removes no ambient sectors, exactly as the note states.
