\subsection{Three Cutoffs and an Ambient Operator Theorem}

For a real smooth function $f$ on the circle, set
$\widehat f_n=(2\pi)^{-1}\int f(x)e^{-inx}\,dx$ and
\[
 \|f\|_{3/2}=\sum_n|\widehat f_n|(1+|n|^{3/2}).
\]
The microscopic quartic operator is
\[
 T_M(f)=\sum_{|n|\le K_M}\widehat f_n C_{M,n,R_M},\qquad
 K_M=\lfloor M/16\rfloor,\quad R_M=\lfloor M/8\rfloor.
\]
Its lift $A_M(f)$ uses the unital CAR refinement, not a one-particle or
many-body corner extension. Each finite lift is bounded self-adjoint;
its norm is not assumed uniformly bounded in $M$.

\textbf{Common-core lemma SC-1.} The algebraic finite-free-energy space
decomposes orthogonally into unitary highest-weight coset modules. At the
lowest unused free grade, positive modes annihilate; diagonalize the
Hermitian $C_0$ there and generate descendants. Adjoint pairing preserves
orthogonal complements. Finite free-grade multiplicity makes this procedure
exhaust the space. The positive norm identity
$\|C_{-1}v\|^2=2h\|v\|^2$ gives nonnegative lowest weights.

The sourced Carpi--Weiner bound is uniform in lowest weight at fixed $c$:
\[
 \|T(f)\xi\|\le r_c\|f\|_{3/2}\|(1+C_0)\xi\|.
\]
It therefore applies to the module sum. The self-adjoint closures and their
resolvents form direct sums, and the finite-free-energy vectors are a common
core. This modulewise argument does not declare the whole ambient sector
sum to be a single projective vacuum representation: its rotation phases
can differ.

\textbf{Theorem SC-2 [structured proof].}
\textbf{Prove} $A_M(f)$ converges to $T(f)$ on that core, in strong
resolvent sense, and through its one-parameter unitaries.
\begin{description}
\item[$\langle1\rangle1$.] On $P_e$, every retained microscopic mode
agrees exactly with its continuum mode once
$R_M>e+K_M$ and $M/2-1/2>e+2R_M$. The displayed schedules meet both
conditions eventually; $M\ge32(e+1)$ suffices.
\item[$\langle1\rangle2$.] Hence the remaining error is only a
continuum Fourier tail, bounded by
\[
 r_c\|(1+C_0)\xi\|
 \sum_{|n|>K_M}|\widehat f_n|(1+|n|^{3/2})\longrightarrow0.
\]
\item[$\langle1\rangle3$.] For core vectors,
\[
 (A_M-i)^{-1}(T-i)\xi-\xi
   =(A_M-i)^{-1}(T-A_M)\xi\longrightarrow0.
\]
The resolvent norms are at most one, and $(T-i)$ maps a graph core densely
into the Hilbert space. Extend the limit by density; similarly at $-i$.
\item[$\langle1\rangle4$.] The self-adjoint strong-resolvent theorem
gives strong convergence of $e^{itA_M}$, uniformly on compact $t$ intervals.
Finite unitary products converge by telescoping and unit norm.
\item[$\langle1\rangle5$.] QED by $\langle1\rangle1$--$\langle1\rangle4$.
\end{description}
This is an ambient microscopic symmetry limit. It does not by itself remove
the extra current and charge sectors or prove positivity of the raw finite
Hamiltonian, which CA-84 disproves.

\subsection{Local Fields in the Descendant OAR Representation}

In the vacuum module of CA-84, grade selection gives the exactly finite
compression
\[
 T_b(f)=P_bT(f)P_b
       =\sum_{|n|\le b}\widehat f_n P_bC_nP_b.
\]
The CAR construction computes every retained matrix element using the safe
schedule of CH-2. A computable smearing supplies only finitely many requested
Fourier coefficients at each stage; no large many-body exponentiation was
performed in the numerical work.

The algebra element $(e^{itT_b(f)},1)$ is represented as identity off $V_b$.
Its strong limit is $e^{itT(f)}$: the zero-extended finite compressions
converge on the essential core, and the preceding resolvent argument applies.
The complementary scalar matters; a bare corner unitary would omit identity
on the remaining Hilbert space.

Let $\mathcal B(I)$ be the sup-norm C*-algebra of sequences generated by
these bounded unitary approximants with real smooth $f$ supported in $I$.
The strong-star limit map is a $*$-homomorphism on this generated algebra.
Its kernel is a closed ideal, and the quotient identifies with the C*-algebra
generated by the limiting stress unitaries. Vacuum GNS weak closures give
the sourced minimal-model Virasoro net and its complete sector data.

This represented field construction is additional to the genuine strict
OAR norm limit $K(\mathcal H_{\rm vac})+\mathbb C1$. Strong-star-null
sequences are not generally norm-null, so the quotient is not relabelled
as the norm-soft inductive limit of the source.

\subsection{The Local Fusion-Space Question}

The finite descendant matrices currently do not retain spatial intervals:
finitely many Fourier moments of functions supported in any nonempty
interval can be independently specified. Nor can refinement preserve every
same-smearing compression exactly, since a smearing can have all low
moments zero and higher moments nonzero.

These facts concern this specific encoding, not global fusion constraints
in general. The next task is to identify a categorical fusion-space basis
and local interactions/refinements that realize the required matrices and
limits. No such identification or bounded-range implementation is claimed
in the present work.
