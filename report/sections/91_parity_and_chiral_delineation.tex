\subsection{Two Corrections Recorded First}

\textbf{(1) The reflection must be complex-linear} [derived; reviewer, REVIEW.md:179--187].  The brief's parity is
``site reflection composed with the pivotal/dagger structure'' (brief:67--68).  Dagger is conjugate-linear
(Penneys:145), and so is \(f\mapsto(f^\vee)^\dagger\) (NOTE.md:641--653).  Remark 91.4 shows that such a reflection
\emph{preserves} each chirality and gives no \(c_L=c_R\) constraint; every result below takes a complex-linear
unitary \(P_r\) as data.  E3's reflection-positivity reflection is conjugate-linear (E3 NOTE.md:355--357), so RP
reflection and chirality-exchanging parity are distinct data (INTEGRATION.md:102--106).
\textbf{(2) Equal ledgers are a hypothesis.}  The note's abstract (NOTE.md:8--10) derives mixed commutation from
locality, two VR-1 certificates and current conservation and \emph{omits} equal anomaly ledgers, which E2-Z
assumes (NOTE.md:295; REVIEW.md:37--42, (M1)).  Here every statement that uses them lists them; a linear parity
supplies them (Theorem 91.1).  Dropping them is open: when the mixed brackets are scalars, the 2-cocycle
condition forces the defect to vanish, but the general case is unproved (REVIEW.md:208--212).

\textbf{Proposition 91.14 (E2-T) [conditional; full; category-free; \(\mathsf R2\) in the fixed representation].}

\subsection{What This Says about Rule 17's Chirality Layer}

The results \emph{delineate}; they do not \emph{restrict}.  The layering principle keeps chiral and full targets
both in scope (AGENTS.md:100--103); 91.10 says exactly which hypotheses a chiral target obtained from a periodic
