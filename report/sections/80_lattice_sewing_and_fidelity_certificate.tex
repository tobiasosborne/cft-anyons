\subsection{Setting and Notation}

\(\Cc\) is unitary fusion with site object \(O\) (CONVENTIONS (r)).  Following (ak), \(H=-\sum_jh_j\),
\(h_j\in\operatorname{End}_\Cc(O\otimes O)\), \(h_j=\sum_\alpha c_\alpha D^{(j)}_\alpha\) in the elementary
tiles \(D_\alpha\); \(e_j=b_jd_j\) is \emph{only} the cup-cap tile.  The \emph{exact tile} is
\(\mathcal T_j(t):=e^{th_j}=\sum_\alpha w_\alpha(t)D^{(j)}_\alpha\); its coefficients \(w_\alpha(t)\) differ
from \(c_\alpha\) (CA-86's first-order weight is \(w_\alpha=\delta\tau\,c_\alpha\)).  The positive bond
energy is \(k_j:=-h_j\), so \(H=\sum_jk_j\), with current \(J_j:=i[k_j,k_{j+1}]\); \(k_j\) is the density
called \(h_{(n,j)}\) in (af) and LC-4.  A cut \(\gamma\) carrying ordered object \(W_\gamma\) has Hilbert
space \(\mathcal H_\gamma=\bigoplus_{a\in\Irr\Cc}\Hom_\Cc(a,W_\gamma)\), all charges and multiplicities
kept; this is not a tensor factorisation across a fusion constraint.  Hermitian forms are linear in the
second slot.  A finite patch \(P\) is a fixed oriented brick graph with input/output cuts, and
\[
  Z_P=\sum_{\Gamma\text{ on }P}\Big(\prod_pw_{p,\alpha_p}\Big)\operatorname{Ev}_\Cc(\Gamma),
\]
internal labels summed and external labels free; with exact tiles there is no \(O(\delta\tau^2)\)
remainder (contrast Lemma 86.1).

\subsection{Exact Cells and Sewing}

\textbf{Lemma 80.1 [derived; D5-S, REVIEW ``confirmed''].}  \textbf{Assume} finite patches \(P,Q\) sharing a
cut \(\gamma\) that runs \emph{along tile legs} (a zigzag through no plaquette) with compatible
orientation, a basis \(\{u_A\}\) of \(\mathcal H_\gamma\) with Gram matrix \(\mathbf G_{AB}=\langle
u_A,u_B\rangle\), and synthesis map \(U\).  \textbf{Prove} \(Z_{Q\circ P}=Z_Q\big(\sum_{AB}\ket{u_A}
(\mathbf G^{-1})_{AB}\bra{u_B}\big)Z_P=Z_QZ_P\), and dual block bases transform by \(F^{-T}\).
\(\langle1\rangle1\). A history on the sewn graph is a pair of histories with a common interface label;
weights multiply and evaluations compose.  BY the definition of \(Z\).
\(\langle1\rangle2\). \(\mathbf G=U^\dagger U\) gives \(U\mathbf G^{-1}U^\dagger=1_{\mathcal H_\gamma}\); summing the
common labels is this insertion.  BY \(\langle1\rangle1\).
\(\langle1\rangle3\). With tree kets \(t_L=Ft_R\), a dual basis \(b_L=\tilde Ub_R\) satisfies \(\tilde UF^T=1\),
so \(\tilde U=F^{-T}=\overline F\) in unitary gauge.  This is LC-9 Lemma FP-D (the continuum statement)
restated at a lattice cut.  QED.
Regroupings of one weighted graph agree; an \(F\)-move changes basis but does not identify two
different weighted brick graphs.

\subsection{Temporal Reflection Positivity}

\textbf{Lemma 80.2 [derived; D5-P, ``confirmed''].}  \textbf{Assume} a temporal reflection cut whose
quotient \(\mathcal H_\gamma\) is spanned by half-patch insertions \(x_r\), and seam operator
\(\Sigma=\sum_\alpha w_\alpha D_\alpha\).  \textbf{Prove} all-insertion reflection positivity holds iff
\(\Sigma\ge0\), iff each charge block \(\Sigma^a=[w_{a,\mu\nu}]\ge0\) in multiplicity matrix units.
\(\langle1\rangle1\). The reflected gluing is \(Q_{rs}=\langle x_r,\Sigma x_s\rangle\), BY Lemma 80.1 and the
dagger reflection.  \(\langle1\rangle2\). \(\sum\bar z_rz_sQ_{rs}=\langle x,\Sigma x\rangle\) with \(x=\sum
z_sx_s\) ranging over \(\mathcal H_\gamma\) by spanning.  \(\langle1\rangle3\). \(\operatorname{End}_\Cc(W)
=\bigoplus_a\operatorname{End}(H_a)\) faithfully, so positivity is blockwise; categorical weights
\(d_a>0\) do not change it.  QED.

\textbf{Corollary 80.3 [derived; coefficient constraints].}  (i) If \(\mathcal T(t)\) is reflection
positive for all \(t\in[0,\varepsilon)\) (spanning as in Lemma 80.2), then \(h=\lim_{t\downarrow0}(\mathcal T(t)-1)/t\) is a limit of
self-adjoint operators, so \(h=h^\dagger\).  (ii) If \(h=h^\dagger\), \(e^{th}>0\) for every real \(t\).
Writing \(D_\alpha^\dagger=\sum_\beta J_{\beta\alpha}D_\beta\), the condition is
\(c_\beta=\sum_\alpha J_{\beta\alpha}\overline{c_\alpha}\) (in matrix units
\(c_{a,\mu\nu}=\overline{c_{a,\nu\mu}}\)).  \textbf{No sign condition \(c_\alpha\ge0\) is necessary}, and
positive coefficients are not sufficient; for \(h=h^\dagger\) a linearised tile \(1+th\) is positive iff
\(\lambda_{\min}(h^a)\ge-1/t\) on every block (\(t>0\)).

\textbf{Lemma 80.4 [derived; D5-R and REVIEW note (e)].}  \textbf{Assume} \(h_j=h_j^\dagger\), mutually
commuting terms within the odd and within the even layer, \(A=\prod_{\mathrm{odd}}e^{th_j}\),
\(B=\prod_{\mathrm{even}}e^{th_j}\), \(t\) real.  \textbf{Prove} \(R=A^{1/2}BA^{1/2}>0\), \(R'(0)=\sum_jh_j=-H\),
and \(R=A^{-1/2}(AB)A^{1/2}\); hence CA-86's row \(U=AB\) is similar to a positive operator and
\(\Tr(AB)^m=\Tr R^m>0\).
\(\langle1\rangle1\). \(A,B>0\): commuting positive factors.  \(\langle1\rangle2\).
\(R=(B^{1/2}A^{1/2})^\dagger(B^{1/2}A^{1/2})\) is invertible, so \(R>0\).  \(\langle1\rangle3\). Product rule
at \(t=0\): two half odd layers give one odd sum.  \(\langle1\rangle4\). Similarity is
\(A^{-1/2}ABA^{1/2}=A^{1/2}BA^{1/2}\).  QED.
At fixed volume \(R^M\to e^{-\beta H}\) as the step shrinks; no uniform-in-volume rate is claimed.
Range-\(r\) densities need an \(r\)-layer palindromic schedule (REVIEW \S5), routine but not written.

\textbf{Lemma 80.5 [derived; D5-G, energy-resolved positivity].}  \textbf{Assume} a finite collar generator
\(E=E^\dagger\ge0\) with spectral projections \(P_\lambda\) and \(X\) mapping external states to the cut.
\textbf{Prove} \(Q(t)=X^\dagger e^{-tE}X=\sum_\lambda e^{-t\lambda}X^\dagger P_\lambda X\) has
\((-1)^kQ^{(k)}(t)\ge0\), and every multi-time kernel \([X_r^\dagger e^{-(t_r+t_s)E}X_s]_{rs}\ge0\).
\(\langle1\rangle1\). Each coefficient is \((P_\lambda X)^\dagger(P_\lambda X)\).  \(\langle1\rangle2\).
\(\lambda^ke^{-t\lambda}\ge0\).  \(\langle1\rangle3\). The kernel's form is \(\|\sum_se^{-t_sE}X_su_s\|^2\).  QED.
Pointwise positivity does not suffice: \(Q(t)=1-\tfrac12e^{-t}>0\) has a negative energy-one coefficient,
and its two-time determinant is \(-\tfrac12(e^{-t_1}-e^{-t_2})^2<0\).

\textbf{Lemma 80.6 [derived finite identities; D5-W].}  For differentiable \(h(s)\),
\(\partial_se^{th(s)}=\int_0^te^{(t-u)h}(\partial_sh)e^{uh}\,du\) (variation of constants), and for
nearest-neighbour \(k_j\) with disjoint-support commutativity
\(i[H,k_j]=J_{j-1}-J_j\) (this restates (af) and LC-4:39).  For a proposed connection with residual
\(W_X=\nabla_XZ\), \(W_X(Z_m\cdots Z_1)=\sum_kZ_m\cdots Z_{k+1}W_X(Z_k)Z_{k-1}\cdots Z_1\) and
\(F_{\mathrm{out}}(X,Y)Z-ZF_{\mathrm{in}}(X,Y)=\nabla_XW_Y-\nabla_YW_X-W_{[X,Y]}\) (anomaly line in
``out'').  For contractions \(\|W_X(Z_m\cdots Z_1)\|\le\sum_k\|W_X(Z_k)\|\): over a growing patch the
\emph{total} residual must vanish; small per-tile error is insufficient.  Conformality, the flux and
improvement equations of LC-4, and scalar curvature are \emph{not} derived.

\textbf{Coverage of CA-75 Link (a).}  (a2) is answered only for cuts along tile legs.  \emph{Not covered:}
straight spatial seams through plaquettes (the case Link (a2) actually poses), the anomaly-line limit, and
\emph{spatial} reflection positivity, whose crossing-kernel test changes with the leg reshuffling
(NOTE.md:231--239).  (a3) gains only the total-residual bookkeeping; (a1) and (a4) are untouched.

\subsection{Defect Operators Do Not Certify Fidelity}

\textbf{Lemma 80.7 [conditional; D5-D, ``conditional-ok''].}  \textbf{Assume} isometric WT-compatible
embeddings \(J_n\), uniformly bounded defects \(D_{a,n}\) with \(D_{\one,n}=1\),
\(D_{a,n}D_{b,n}=\sum_cN_{ab}^cD_{c,n}\), \(D_{a,n}^\dagger=D_{\bar a,n}\), and summable
\(\|D_{a,n+1}J_n-J_nD_{a,n}\|\le\epsilon_{a,n}\) (likewise for adjoints).  \textbf{Prove} strong limits
\(D_a\) exist and obey the same relations; they commute with the limiting dynamics if the bounded
evolutions and their commutators converge strongly.  \emph{Sketch:} telescoping with tail
\(\|v\|\sum_{k\ge n}\epsilon_{a,k}\) on the dense union, extension by uniform bounds, and strong convergence
of products of uniformly bounded strongly convergent sequences (NOTE.md:356--363).

\textbf{Proposition 80.8 [derived, negative; scalar falsifier].}  For every unitary fusion \(\Cc\), \(D_a:=d_a1\)
satisfies every hypothesis-level relation of Lemma 80.7 (by \(d_ad_b=\sum_cN_{ab}^cd_c\) and
\(d_{\bar a}=d_a\)), commutes with every \(H\), and carries no charge separation; for \(|\Irr\Cc|>1\) it is a
nonfaithful representation of the fusion algebra.  Hence convergent fusion operators, fusion relations and
commutation with \(H\) cannot certify fidelity for any \(\Cc\).  Actual sectors need localised, locally
normal, transportable endomorphisms with convergent junctions; Gram lower bounds give only injectivity, not
fullness (NOTE.md:374--381).

\subsection{The Two-Sided Certificate}

Fix four cyclically ordered intervals covering \(S^1\) up to endpoints;
\(\mathcal N=\Alg(I_1)\vee\Alg(I_3)\subset\mathcal M=(\Alg(I_2)\vee\Alg(I_4))'\), and
\(\mu_\Alg=[\mathcal M:\mathcal N]\), the minimal Pimsner--Popa index (KLM:430--443).  Let \(\mathsf B\) be the
declared chiral UMTC, \(\operatorname{Dim}\mathsf B=\sum_id_i^2\).

\textbf{Theorem 80.9 [conditional; D5-F in the review's repaired form].}  \textbf{Assume}
(F1) the OAR limit is a completely rational conformal net \(\Alg\) (irreducible, strongly additive, split,
with modular PCT; none of this follows from WT-1/VR-1);
(F2) lattice endpoints give a fully faithful unitary braided tensor functor
\(\Phi:\mathsf B\to\mathrm{DHR}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\Alg)\) with \(d(\Phi(i))=d_i\), preserving twists, duality
and orientation;
(F3\('\)) there is a normal conditional expectation \(E:\mathcal M\to\mathcal N\) with
\(E(x)\ge(\operatorname{Dim}\mathsf B)^{-1}x\) for \(x\in\mathcal M_+\).
\textbf{Prove} \(\Phi\) is a braided equivalence onto the complete finite-sector category and
\(\mu_\Alg=\operatorname{Dim}\mathsf B\).
\(\langle1\rangle1\). \(\mu_\Alg\le[\mathcal M:\mathcal N]_E\le\operatorname{Dim}\mathsf B\) BY (F3\('\)) and
the PP definition.  \(\langle1\rangle2\). BY (F1) and finiteness, \(\mu_\Alg=\sum_\rho d(\rho)^2\) over all
irreducible sectors, and every sector is a finite direct sum (KLM:1719--1729, :1899--1900).
\(\langle1\rangle3\). The image of \(\Phi\) contributes exactly \(\operatorname{Dim}\mathsf B\): simples stay
simple (\(\operatorname{End}=\mathbb C\)), distinct simples stay inequivalent, dimensions are preserved, BY (F2).
\(\langle1\rangle4\). An extra simple adds \(d(\rho)^2>0\), contradicting \(\langle1\rangle1\)--\(\langle1\rangle3\);
so \(\Phi\) is essentially surjective, hence a braided equivalence.  QED.  Normalised \(S\) and twists then
match by ribbon traces; the character \(T\) needs the declared central-charge lift as well.

\textbf{The gap, explicitly.}  The note's checkable hypothesis was finite-dimensional:
\(\mathcal N_k\subset\mathcal M_k\) exhausting \(\mathcal N,\mathcal M\) with
\(E_k\ge(\operatorname{Dim}\mathsf B+\varepsilon_k)^{-1}\mathrm{id}\), passed to the limit by KLM's monotone
exhaustion (Prop.\ p3(a), KLM:507--527).  KLM proves the increasing case ``after taking commutants''
(KLM:528--530), which uses commutant-invariance of the index.  That fails for Pimsner--Popa constants of
finite-dimensional inclusions: \(\mathbb C1\subset M_n\) has index \(n\), while its commutant inclusion
\(1\otimes M_n\subset M_n\otimes M_n\) on \(\mathbb C^n\otimes\mathbb C^n\) has index \(n^2\)
(\path{checks/review_pp_constant.py}, exact for \(n=2,3\)).  The finite form also assumes neither
compatible \(E_k\) nor a preserved state, so no normal limit expectation is built.  \emph{Theorem 80.9
therefore holds only under (F3\('\)) or factor approximants; a finite-algebra positive-map test is
currently not a sufficient check.}  No counterexample to the conclusion is known; the gap is in the route.
\emph{Repair routes} (any one suffices; REVIEW.md:157--163): (i) (F3) for infinite-factor approximants;
(ii) compatible \(E_k\) preserving a faithful normal state, yielding a normal limit \(E\ge\lambda\,\mathrm{id}\);
(iii) a complete PP bound plus a sourced commutant lemma (source not local: Law~1 gap).  By Lemma 80.10 the
state in (ii) cannot be the vacuum.  The pure operator-algebra lemma behind (ii) is the first attackable
target (INTEGRATION.md:121--125, a \emph{proposal}).  Exhaustion of \(\mathcal M\) itself is the separate
conditional repair estimate D5-A (NOTE.md:458--504), valid with probes exactly in
\(\Alg(I_2)\vee\Alg(I_4)\); its hypothesis nearly restates its conclusion (REVIEW.md:167--171).

\textbf{Lemma 80.10 [derived, negative; D5-V].}  \textbf{Assume} \(\mathcal N\subset\mathcal M\),
\(\Omega\) cyclic for \(\mathcal N\) and separating for \(\mathcal M\), and an \(\mathcal N\)-bimodular
expectation \(E\) preserving \(\omega=\langle\Omega,\cdot\,\Omega\rangle\).  \textbf{Prove} \(\mathcal M=\mathcal N\).
\(\langle1\rangle1\). \(\langle n\Omega,E(x)\Omega\rangle=\omega(E(n^*x))=\langle n\Omega,x\Omega\rangle\).
\(\langle1\rangle2\). Cyclicity gives \(E(x)\Omega=x\Omega\); separation gives \(E(x)=x\).  QED.  By Reeh--Schlieder
the hypotheses hold for the four-interval inclusion, so an annular expectation preserves the vacuum only if
\(\mu_\Alg=1\).

\textbf{Lemma 80.11 [conditional; D5-M, valid for \(\mathsf B=\Cc\) only].}  \textbf{Assume} resolved modular
covariance with multiplicities \(M_{ij}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), \(M_{\one\one}=1\) (M1); trace-norm convergence of
lattice traces including defect insertions (M2); defects forming a unital \(*\)-representation of
\(\mathsf B\)'s fusion algebra with Verlinde \(S\) (M3).  \textbf{Prove} \(MS=SM\), \(MT=TM\); defect
eigencharacters are \(\lambda_i(a)=S_{ai}/S_{\one i}\) via projections
\(p_i=S_{\one i}\sum_a\overline{S_{ai}}D_a\); every \(i\) occurs iff the representation is faithful.
Multiplicities are \emph{not} fixed (proof NOTE.md:545--561; self-adjointness of \(p_i\) uses
\(S_{\bar ai}=\overline{S_{ai}}\), \(S_{\one i}>0\)).  For \(\mathsf B=Z(\Cc)\) or fusion-only \(\Cc\) a
tube-algebra version is needed and is not written.

\textbf{Lemma 80.12 [conditional; D5-H, sharp].}  Under \(c>1\), nonnegative weights, a weight-zero vacuum,
positive characters with \(S\)-covariance and \(S_{\one i}>0\), the vacuum carrier is not a finite sum of
Virasoro highest-weight families: PBW gives \(\log\chi_\one(i/t)\le\pi t/12+O(1)\), against
\(\ge\pi ct/12+\log S_{\one\one}\) (NOTE.md:586--599).  It concurs with D4-G and sharpens LC-12 \S5
(\(c>12/\pi^2\) there) to \(c>1\).

\textbf{Why selection is indispensable.}  (F2) with \(\mathsf B=\Cc\) is a strong demand on \((O,h)\): the
category defining a lattice model and the category of the continuum primaries typically differ (AFM-J:123--128,
AFM-S:775).  A selection principle (CA-78; D2) must supply it; nothing in this shard does.

\textbf{Conjecture 80.C [conjectural; D5-C].}  Every computably presented UMTC admits, with some compatible
declared enrichment, a local lattice family whose OAR/KS limit satisfies (F1)--(F3\('\)) with
\(\mathsf B=\Cc\).  \emph{Review verdict} (REVIEW.md:26--28, :182--211; INTEGRATION.md:115--117): it contains the
open UMTC realisation problem (Bischoff2018:364--372) plus the whole analytic layer; it is degenerate for
\(\Cc=\mathrm{Vec}\) unless \(c>0\) is required; its uniformity clause is trivialised by proof search; it
nearly duplicates D6-C.  It is not a pipeline step.

\textbf{Open decision: chiral versus full target (PLAN.md D1).}  D6's headline asks a 1D tower for a
\emph{chiral} limit net, while D2, D4 and D5 target full two-chiral theories with a tested chiral subnet
(D6 REVIEW.md:20--24, :74--100).  No local source settles whether a 1D lattice can have a chiral OAR limit;
Theorem 80.9 is stated for whichever chiral subnet the declared target names, and neither periodic boundary
conditions nor commutation with \(H\) chooses it.

\subsection{What This Does Not Claim}

No critical Hamiltonian, convergent cell family, chiral or full OAR limit, endpoint functor, or index bound
is constructed for any \(\Cc\).  Spatial reflection positivity, straight spatial seams, OS reconstruction,
conformality of the Ward residual, and a finite-dimensional route to \(\mu_\Alg\le\operatorname{Dim}\mathsf B\)
remain open.  Matching \(S,T\), fusion dimensions or closed-loop spectra is not fidelity.
