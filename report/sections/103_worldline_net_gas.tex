\textbf{Theorem 103.1 [derived; any; \(\mathsf L0\)+(\(O,\eta,h\), decorated tile basis); finite, \(\to\mathsf R3\)] (NOTE:\allowbreak 54--136; REVIEW:\allowbreak 64--82).}

\textbf{Negative 103.9 [negative; any; \(\mathsf L0\); finite] (NOTE:\allowbreak 324--397; REVIEW:\allowbreak 130--147).}\newline
H1(d)(i)'s ``nonnegative coefficients iff nonnegative closed weights'' fails both ways.
\emph{Necessity} fails for every self-dual raw cup: \(h_\pm=\pm(b+b^\dagger)\), \(s=t\sqrt d\),
\[ e^{th_\pm}=1+(\cosh s-1)(P_{\emptyset\emptyset}+e/d)\pm d^{-1/2}\sinh s\,(b+b^\dagger); \]
\(h_-\) has negative cup/cap coefficients yet
closed histories have equally many births and deaths, so even sign counts (a gauge artefact, \(U=P_0+iP_1\)).
\emph{Sufficiency} fails for every pseudoreal \(X\) (\(\kappa_X=-1\)) with raw \(v,v^\dagger\): \((v^\dagger\otimes1)(1\otimes v)=\kappa_X1_X\)
and the W-shaped single component (two births, two deaths, nearest-neighbour on four sites) evaluates to

The separating hypothesis is \(\kappa=+1\), or proper oriented cups with their actual coefficients. CA-86 Cor.~86.2 drops it.
