\subsection{Edge-Density Input}

On an open square patch, use oriented horizontal and vertical edges:
\[
  h^a_{r,r+\hat a}
  =
  \sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^3
  c^a_{\alpha\beta}\,
  \sigma_r^\alpha\sigma_{r+\hat a}^\beta,
  \qquad a\in\{x,y\}.
\]
Let \(m_e\) be the midpoint of edge \(e\), and write
\[
  H=\sum_e h_e,\qquad
  K_a=\sum_e m_e^a h_e.
\]
The ramp-current candidate is
\[
  P_a:=i[H,K_a]
  =
  \sum_{\{e,f\}}
  (m_f^a-m_e^a)i[h_e,h_f],
\]
where only overlapping edge pairs contribute.  This is the direct
higher-dimensional analogue of CA-13's scalar-ramp formula.

\subsection{Patch Diagnostics}

The smallest useful checks are local:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
1.&\text{three-site lines for straight }x\text{ and }y\text{ currents},\\
2.&2\times2\text{ plaquettes for corner commutators and }C_4\text{ covariance},\\
3.&3\times3\text{ open patches for }P_x,P_y\text{ and }i[H,P_a],\\
4.&4\times4\text{ or larger patches for bulk-projected }i[P_x,P_y].
\end{array}
\]
Boundary-supported residuals should be projected away or recorded separately.
The diagnostic object is the interior Pauli-string residual, not raw equality
on the whole open patch.

\subsection{Proposed Rejection Filters}

\begin{description}
\item[Current nontriviality.]
Both \(P_x\) and \(P_y\) should be nonzero in the bulk for a nontrivial
\(2+1\)-dimensional Poincare candidate.

\item[Translation conservation.]
The bulk residual \(i[H,P_a]\) should vanish or be controlled in the intended
finite algebra.  A nonzero interior Pauli-string residual is an early rejection
witness for exact finite bulk closure.

\item[Translation commutativity.]
The bulk part of \(i[P_x,P_y]\) should vanish or be a named boundary/irrelevant
term before the model is promoted.

\item[Boost-translation bracket.]
After normalisation, the residual
\[
  i[P_b,K_a]-\delta_{ab}H
\]
is the direct \(2+1\)-dimensional Poincare diagnostic.  This is strong and must
be treated as a finite filter, not a theorem.

\item[Isotropy.]
For scalar qubits with no internal spatial action, a microscopic \(90^\circ\)
rotation should relate horizontal and vertical edge data after endpoint
reversal.  Obvious decoupled stacks, such as \(c^y=0\), fail this filter.
\end{description}

\subsection{Rotation Is Not Free}

A rotation candidate needs a local momentum-density split
\[
  P_a=\sum_v p_a(v)
\]
before one can write
\[
  J_{xy}=\sum_v(x_vp_y(v)-y_vp_x(v)).
\]
CA-13 already marks this as extra data.  A plausible first convention is to
assign each edge-pair current to the shared vertex, but that convention must be
written and tested before rotation closure is claimed.  Only then should one
check
\[
  i[H,J_{xy}],\qquad
  i[P_x,J_{xy}]-P_y,\qquad
  i[P_y,J_{xy}]+P_x.
\]

\subsection{Rule-Out Classes}

The proposal immediately targets four classes:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
1.&\text{fully on-site Hamiltonians: }P_x=P_y=0,\\
2.&\text{commuting classical edge densities: all ramp currents vanish,}\\
3.&\text{decoupled stacks: one momentum component vanishes,}\\
4.&\text{anisotropic edge data: fail the scalar-qubit }C_4\text{ filter.}
\end{array}
\]
None of these rejections is a continuum theorem.  They are finite algebraic
filters for the specific candidate-generator route.
