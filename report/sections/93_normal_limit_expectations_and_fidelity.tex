\subsection{Scoped Notation}

All inclusions are unital, in \(\BH(\mathcal H)\).  For a normal conditional expectation \(E:\mathcal M\to\mathcal N\) the
\emph{cost} is \(K(E):=\inf\{\beta\ge1:E(x)\ge\beta^{-1}x\ \forall x\in\mathcal M_+\}=[\mathcal M:\mathcal N]_E\) (KLM:430--443;
the infimum is attained when finite), and \(q(\mathcal N\subset\mathcal M):=\inf_EK(E)\).  For the four-interval inclusion
of (al), \(\mu_\Alg\le K(E)\) for every \(E\); KLM's minimum runs over all expectations, which does not affect this
inequality (REVIEW.md:55--58).  A \emph{bounded exhaustion} of \(\mathcal M\) by increasing \(\mathcal M_k\subset\mathcal M\)
means: every \(x\in\mathcal M\) is the strong\(^*\) limit of a norm-bounded sequence \(x_k\in\mathcal M_k\); it is a
hypothesis, not a density theorem (NOTE.md:58--63).  \(\psi\) is an \emph{auxiliary} normal state, never the physical
vacuum (Lemma 80.10); \(\varphi:=\psi|_{\mathcal N}\).  \(D:=\operatorname{Dim}\Cc=\sum_id_i^2\).

\subsection{The Normal Limit Expectation}

\textbf{Lemma 93.1 [derived; E5-G; any; category-free; \(\to\mathsf R4\)] (positive-graph passage).}
\textbf{Assume} a normal expectation \(F:\mathcal M\to\mathcal N\), normal expectations \(E_k:\mathcal M_k\to\mathcal N_k\)
inside \(\mathcal N\subset\mathcal M\), \(b=\liminf_kK(E_k)<\infty\) realised along \(k_j\), and for each
\(x\in\mathcal M_+\) bounded positive \(x_j\in\mathcal M_{k_j}\) with \(x_j\to x\) and \(E_{k_j}(x_j)\to F(x)\) ultraweakly.
\textbf{Prove} \(K(F)\le b\).
\(\langle1\rangle1\). \(E_{k_j}(x_j)-K(E_{k_j})^{-1}x_j\ge0\), BY definition of \(K\).
\(\langle1\rangle2\). Pairing with normal positive functionals and passing to the limit gives \(F(x)-b^{-1}x\ge0\),
BY boundedness and closedness of the positive cone.  QED (NOTE.md:84--90).

\textbf{Lemma 93.2 [derived; E5-L; any; category-free; \(\to\mathsf R4\)] (normal limit expectation).}
\textbf{Assume}
(i) increasing \(\mathcal N_k\subset\mathcal M_k\) with \(\mathcal N_k\subset\mathcal N\), \(\mathcal M_k\subset\mathcal M\), bounded
exhaustions of both \(\mathcal N\) and \(\mathcal M\);
(ii) normal conditional expectations \(E_k:\mathcal M_k\to\mathcal N_k\), \emph{not} assumed compatible;
(iii) a normal state \(\psi\) on \(\mathcal M\), faithful on \(\mathcal N\), with \(\psi\circ E_k=\psi|_{\mathcal M_k}\) for all \(k\);
(iv) \(b:=\liminf_kK(E_k)<\infty\).
\textbf{Prove} there is a unique \(\psi\)-preserving normal conditional expectation \(E:\mathcal M\to\mathcal N\); it satisfies
\(E(x)\ge b^{-1}x\) on \(\mathcal M_+\) (so \(q(\mathcal N\subset\mathcal M)\le b\)), and \(E\) and \(\psi\) are faithful.  (i)--(iii)
alone already construct \(E\).
\(\langle1\rangle1\). For \(n\in\mathcal N_r\), \(k\ge r\), \(a\in\mathcal M_k\), BY bimodularity and (iii):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:93-pairing}
  \psi\big(n^*E_k(a)\big)=\psi\big(E_k(n^*a)\big)=\psi(n^*a).
\end{equation}
\(\langle1\rangle2\). For bounded \(a_k\to x\) strong\(^*\), every ultraweak cluster point \(y\in\mathcal N\) of \(E_k(a_k)\) obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:93-Q}
  \varphi(n^*y)=\psi(n^*x)\qquad\forall n\in\mathcal N,
\end{equation}
BY \eqref{eq:93-pairing}, normality of \(\psi\), compactness of the ball and bounded exhaustion of \(\mathcal N\).  If \(y,z\) both
satisfy \eqref{eq:93-Q}, take \(n=y-z\): \(\varphi((y-z)^*(y-z))=0\), so \(y=z\) BY faithfulness of \(\varphi\).  Define \(E(x):=y\);
it is independent of the approximants, and every bounded approximating sequence has \(E_k(a_k)\to E(x)\) ultraweakly.
\(\langle1\rangle3\). \(E\) is linear, unital, completely positive and fixes \(\mathcal N\): approximate the entries of
\(X^{1/2}\) for positive \(X\in M_m(\mathcal M)\) and take positive ultraweak limits (fixed matrix size).
\(\langle1\rangle4\). \(\psi\circ E=\psi\) (\(n=1\) in \eqref{eq:93-Q}).  \emph{Normality:} if \(x_\alpha\uparrow x\), then
\(y:=\sup_\alpha E(x_\alpha)\le E(x)\) and \(\varphi(E(x)-y)=\psi(x)-\lim_\alpha\psi(x_\alpha)=0\), so \(y=E(x)\).  This is the step
finite-stage normality cannot supply (Example 93.5).
\(\langle1\rangle5\). Bimodularity passes from \(\mathcal N_r\) to \(\mathcal N\) BY \(\langle1\rangle2\) and normality.
\(\langle1\rangle6\). Uniqueness: any \(\psi\)-preserving normal expectation satisfies \eqref{eq:93-Q}; apply \(\langle1\rangle2\).
\(\langle1\rangle7\). Bounded positive approximants along a subsequence realising \(b\) satisfy the hypotheses of Lemma 93.1
BY \(\langle1\rangle2\); hence \(E(x)\ge b^{-1}x\).
\(\langle1\rangle8\). \(E(x)=0\), \(x\ge0\) forces \(x=0\) BY \(\langle1\rangle7\); \(\psi(x)=0\) forces \(E(x)=0\).  QED
(full proof NOTE.md:116--187; every step graded valid, REVIEW.md:67--86).
\emph{Remarks.}  Neither commutants (KLM:507--556) nor complete positivity of \(E_k-\lambda\,\mathrm{id}\) is used;
type-I or nonfactor approximants cause no difficulty.  Increasing \(\mathcal M_k\) is not used in \(\langle1\rangle1\)
(REVIEW.md:69).

\textbf{Corollary 93.3 [derived; E5-J; any; category-free].}  Under Lemma 93.2, in the GNS representation of \(\psi\)
with vector \(\Omega_\psi\) (not the vacuum), the projections onto \(\overline{\mathcal M_k\Omega_\psi}\) converge strongly to
\(1\), the Jones projections \(e_k\) onto \(\overline{\mathcal N_k\Omega_\psi}\) converge strongly to \(e\) onto
\(\overline{\mathcal N\Omega_\psi}\), and \(E_k(x_k)\to E(x)\) strong\(^*\) for bounded \(x_k\to x\) strong\(^*\).  Jones convergence is a
\emph{consequence}, not a substitute for normality or exhaustion (NOTE.md:195--227; REVIEW.md:88--93).

\textbf{Proposition 93.4 [derived; E5-N; any; category-free] (compatible case, exact criterion).}  \textbf{Assume}
(i), (ii) of Lemma 93.2 with \(E_l|_{\mathcal M_k}=E_k\) (\(l\ge k\)), \(E_0\) the induced map on
\(\bigcup_k\mathcal M_k\), and a faithful normal state \(\varphi\) on \(\mathcal N\) (exists on a separable Hilbert space,
REVIEW.md:100--101).  \textbf{Prove} \(E_0\) extends to a normal expectation \(\mathcal M\to\mathcal N\) iff \(\varphi\circ E_0\)
extends to a normal positive functional on \(\mathcal M\); and if \(b<\infty\), then \(K(E)=\sup_kK(E_k)=\lim_kK(E_k)=b\).
\(\langle1\rangle1\). Necessity: \(\psi:=\varphi\circ E\).  \(\langle1\rangle2\). Sufficiency: the extension \(\psi\) restricts to
\(\varphi\) on \(\mathcal N\) and is preserved by every \(E_k\); apply Lemma 93.2; constant sequences show \(E\) extends \(E_0\).
\(\langle1\rangle3\). Restriction gives \(K(E_k)\uparrow\) and \(\sup_kK(E_k)\le K(E)\); Lemma 93.2 gives \(\le b\).  QED (NOTE.md:242--252).
\emph{Selection remark.}  If only minimal stage constants \(q_k=q(\mathcal N_k\subset\mathcal M_k)\) are known, a selection
certificate (O) is needed: expectations with \(K(E_{k_j})\le q_{k_j}+\delta_j\), \(\delta_j\to0\), along a subsequence realising
\(\liminf q_k\) (NOTE.md:260--269).  The brief's literal minimal-constant form without (O) is \emph{conjectural}: neither
proved nor refuted (REVIEW.md:16--17, :212).

\subsection{Sharpness of the Hypotheses}

\textbf{Example 93.5 [derived, negative; E5-X1, verified].}  Let \(D_0,D_1\subset\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N}\) be the eventually-0 and
eventually-1 sequences, \(\mathcal H=\ell^2(D_0\sqcup D_1)\), \(\mathcal M_k\) the functions of the first \(k\) bits on each copy
independently, \(\mathcal N_k\) the same function on both copies, \(E_k(f_0,f_1)=\big(\tfrac{f_0+f_1}2,\tfrac{f_0+f_1}2\big)\).  The
\(E_k\) are compatible and normal with \(K(E_k)=q_k=2\), and both exhaust \(\mathcal N=\mathcal M=\ell^\infty(D_0\sqcup D_1)\)
boundedly; yet \(E_0(P_0)=\tfrac12\ne P_0\) for \(P_0=(1,0)\), so \emph{no} expectation extends \(E_0\).  The failure is of
normality: \((0,f_k)\downarrow0\) while \(E_k(0,f_k)\downarrow p_d/2\ne0\) (NOTE.md:273--317; REVIEW.md:105--114).  It refutes
extension of the maps, not index semicontinuity (the limit has \(q=1\le2\)).

\textbf{Example 93.6 [derived, negative; E5-X2].}  \(\mathcal N=\mathbb C1\subset\mathcal M=M_m(\mathbb C)\), \(m\ge2\), on
\(L^2(\mathcal M,\tau)\), with \(\mathcal N_k=\mathcal M_k=\mathbb C1\): the stage Jones projections \emph{equal} \(e_{\mathcal N}\) and the stage
cost is \(1\), but \(q(\mathcal N\subset\mathcal M)=m\).  Jones convergence without ambient exhaustion underestimates the index
(NOTE.md:319--334).  The review notes X2 drops a hypothesis the brief already contained; no counterexample to the
index inequality under full exhaustion without a normal state is known; the review sketches why none exists in the
abelian case, and its noncommutative attempts failed (REVIEW.md:118--123, :176--184; open problem NOTE.md:613--614).

\textbf{Example 93.7 [derived; added by the review] (the bound is attained).}  For the hyperfinite factor, \(R\otimes1\subset
R\otimes M_n\) with \(\mathcal N_k=M_{2^k}\otimes1\subset\mathcal M_k=M_{2^k}\otimes M_n\), \(E_k=\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{tr}\) and \(\psi\) the
trace: the hypotheses of Lemma 93.2 and Proposition 93.4 hold and \(K(E_k)=n\min(2^k,n)\uparrow n^2=[R\otimes M_n:R\otimes1]\)
(REVIEW.md:168--171; \path{checks/review_raw_output.txt}:6).  Lemma 93.2 is not vacuous for genuine subfactors.

\subsection{The Two-Sided Fidelity Theorem at its Lowest Layer}

Let \(\mathcal N=\Alg(I_1)\vee\Alg(I_3)\subset\mathcal M=(\Alg(I_2)\vee\Alg(I_4))'\) be the four-interval inclusion of (al).

\textbf{Theorem 93.8 [derived implication; E5-F with layer lowered by REVIEW \S4; chiral; \(\mathsf L0{+}\mathsf L4\) /
\(\mathsf L1{+}\mathsf L4\); \(\mathsf R4\) given \(\mathsf R3\)].}  \textbf{Assume}
(F1) \(\Alg\) is a completely rational conformal net on a separable Hilbert space (this is \(\mathsf R3\), not supplied here);
(F2\(_0\)) \(\Cc\) is unitary fusion and \(\Phi:\Cc\to\mathrm{DHR}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\Alg)\) is a fully faithful unitary tensor functor;
(F3\(''\)) the hypotheses of Lemma 93.2 (or of Proposition 93.4) hold for the four-interval inclusion with \(b\le D\).
\textbf{Prove} (a) \(\mu_\Alg=D\), \(\Phi\) is a unitary tensor equivalence onto \(\mathrm{DHR}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\Alg)\), and \(\Alg\) has no
further irreducible sectors; (b) if \(\Cc\) is braided and \(\Phi\) braided (\(\mathsf L1\)), \(\Phi\) is a braided equivalence and
\(\Cc\) is \emph{modular}.
\(\langle1\rangle1\). \(\mu_\Alg\le K(E)\le b\le D\), BY Lemma 93.2 and KLM:430--443.
\(\langle1\rangle2\). \(\mu_\Alg=\sum_\rho d(\rho)^2\) over all irreducible finite sectors, BY KLM:1719--1729 (c21), whose modular
PCT hypothesis is automatic for conformal nets (KLM:782--792); every sector is a direct sum of finite-dimensional ones
(KLM:1899--1900).
\(\langle1\rangle3\). The image of \(\Phi\) contributes exactly \(D\): simples stay simple and pairwise inequivalent (full
faithfulness), and \(d(\Phi(i))=\mathrm{FPdim}(i)=d_i\) \emph{automatically}, since statistical dimension is the
Frobenius--Perron dimension (B:620--626; REVIEW.md:197--199).  No dimension-preservation hypothesis is needed.
\(\langle1\rangle4\). An irreducible sector outside the image adds \(d(\rho)^2>0\), contradicting \(\langle1\rangle1\)--\(\langle1\rangle3\);
\(\Phi\) is essentially surjective; the inverse is chosen unitary (NOTE.md:376--377).  No braiding is used (REVIEW.md:200--201).
\(\langle1\rangle5\). At \(\mathsf L1\) a braided equivalence results; \(\mathrm{DHR}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\Alg)\) is nondegenerate BY KLM:1861--1863
(c23), hence so is \(\Cc\).  Twists and ribbon traces for the canonical positive spherical structure are preserved
(B:617--645; REVIEW.md:128--131).  QED.
\emph{Scope.}  For a non-braidable \(\Cc\) (F2\(_0\)) is unsatisfiable (the image would inherit a braiding), so 93.8 is vacuous
there (REVIEW.md:165--167).  \(T\), characters and a central-charge lift remain \(\mathsf L3\) data (CONVENTIONS (u)).
The chiral tag must be read with E2's caveat: a parity-paired KS limit carries both stress families, so a chiral target
needs parity breaking or is the left chiral subnet of a full limit (INTEGRATION.md:97--100).

\subsection{The Full-Setting Corollary and a False Reading}

\textbf{Corollary 93.9 [derived; E5-D; full, named doubled chiral net; \(\mathsf L2{+}\mathsf L4\); \(\mathsf R4\) for that net].}
\textbf{Assume} Theorem 93.8 at \(\mathsf L2\) (\(\Cc\) modular).  \textbf{Prove} KLM's net \(\Alg\otimes\Alg^{\mathrm o}\) has
\(\mathrm{DHR}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\Alg\otimes\Alg^{\mathrm o})\simeq\Cc\boxtimes\Cc^{\mathrm{rev}}\simeq Z(\Cc)\), of dimension \(D^2\).
\(\langle1\rangle1\). Irreducible sectors are exactly \(\rho_i\otimes\rho_j^{\mathrm o}\) (KLM:969--972, :1518--1559, l17, c18);
intertwiners and exchanges factor, the right copy with reversed braiding \(\varepsilon^{\mathrm o}=j(\varepsilon)^*\)
(KLM:1089--1092; B:643--645).  \(\langle1\rangle2\). \(Z(\Cc)\simeq\Cc\boxtimes\Cc^{\mathrm{rev}}\) iff modular (B:660--664);
\(\sum_{ij}(d_id_j)^2=D^2\).  QED (NOTE.md:407--419).
\emph{Which net.}  KLM's c18 concerns their \emph{one-dimensional} net \(\Alg\otimes\Alg^{\mathrm o}\) (time-zero picture); the
corresponding statement for the 2D light-ray product \(\Alg\otimes\bar\Alg\) on \(\mathbb R\times\mathbb R\) is not locally sourced
(REVIEW.md:138--140): a Law~1 gap, recorded, not filled.

\textbf{Proposition 93.10 [derived, negative].}  For modular \(\Cc\ne\mathrm{Vec}\) realised as in 93.8, the canonical
(diagonal) Longo--Rehren extension \(\mathcal B_2\supset\Alg\otimes\Alg^{\mathrm o}\) (KLM:1086--1098) has \emph{trivial} finite
DHR category: by KLM:1578--1587 (p18) and :1861--1879 (c23, \(\mu_{\mathcal B}=1\)) it has no nontrivial bounded-localised
finite-index endomorphism, and double-cone sectors restrict to time-zero interval sectors by cofinality (NOTE.md:425--440).
Since \((a,\one)\) is a nonunit simple of \(Z(\Cc)\), \emph{``\(\mathrm{DHR}\) of the full diagonal extension is \(Z(\Cc)\)'' is false
as stated} (NOTE.md:441--444; REVIEW.md:20--21).  Correct readings: \(Z(\Cc)\) is the sector category of the doubled chiral
net, or the centre of the diagonal theory's defect category, which is \(\Cc\) itself (FRS:641--646; NOTE.md:446--450);
a general full extension declares its own bimodule category (B:3323--3337).

\subsection{What a Lattice Construction Must Supply}

\textbf{Lemma 93.11 [conditional; E5-EP; chiral; \(\mathsf L2{+}\mathsf L4\); \(\to\)(F2), \(\to\mathsf R4\) given \(\mathsf R3\)].}  Finite charged endpoint operators, junction
matrices for bases of \(\Hom_\Cc(a,b)\), tensorators and conjugate pairs, with bounded product/adjoint limits that are
unital, multiplicative, locally normal, localised and transportable; identity errors \(\to0\) (both \(U_k^*U_k\to1\) and
\(U_kU_k^*\to1\)); limiting conjugate pairs proved \emph{standard}; and a fullness certificate (bounded coefficient
approximation of every limiting intertwiner), imply the functor of (F2) (NOTE.md:458--492).  The review notes the fullness
certificate nearly restates fullness (REVIEW.md:151--153).

\textbf{Lemma 93.12 [conditional; E5-AN; chiral; \(\mathsf L2{+}\mathsf L4\); \(\to\)(F3\('')\)].}  Represented finite inclusions \(\mathcal N_k\subset\mathcal M_k\)
with \(\mathcal N_k\subset\mathcal N\), \(\mathcal M_k\subset\mathcal M\), bounded exhaustions of \emph{both}, matrix expectations with
\(E_k(x)\ge b_k^{-1}x\) and \(\liminf b_k\le D\), and a common normal non-vacuum state (or criterion 93.4), give (F3\('')\) and
Corollary 93.3 (NOTE.md:494--522).  The literal inclusions must come from the inductive-limit embedding: an extra
obligation (REVIEW.md:154--156).  No commutant and scaling limit may be interchanged.

\textbf{Lemma 93.13 [conditional; E5-ALL; chiral; \(\mathsf L2{+}\mathsf L4\); \(\mathsf R3\to\mathsf R4\)].}  If one lattice/OAR system has an identified completely
rational conformal limit net and 93.11, 93.12 hold in that representation, Theorem 93.8 applies (NOTE.md:524--532).  It
derives no \(\mathsf R1\)--\(\mathsf R3\) and no convergence to \(\mathcal B_2\).

\textbf{Non-vacuity caveat.}  No instance of Lemma 93.2's hypotheses is known for a type \(\mathrm{III}_1\) four-interval
inclusion.  That inclusion is irreducible with finite index, so its expectation is unique and \(K(E)=\mu_\Alg\)
(REVIEW.md:56--58); certifying costs must therefore have \(\liminf K(E_k)=\mu_\Alg\) \emph{exactly} (and \(\lim\), in the
compatible case 93.4): a sharp lattice demand (REVIEW.md:173--175).  Without an instance, 93.8 is a correct
implication with no known application (INTEGRATION.md:121--123).

\textbf{Next lemma [proposal; any; category-free, then \(\mathsf L0{+}\mathsf L4\)] (annular approximant lemma).}  For a sourced
completely rational net, construct increasing finite-dimensional \(\mathcal N_k\subset\mathcal M_k\) inside the four-interval
inclusion with bounded exhaustion of both, a non-vacuum normal state preserved by the \(E_k\), and \(K(E_k)\to\mu_\Alg\);
equivalently, show the minimal-cost expectations of some exhausting family preserve one normal state
(INTEGRATION.md:114--123).

\textbf{Effect on CA-80.}  This closes route (ii) of CA-80's ``The gap, explicitly'' (CA-80:198--202), with a common
normal state replacing compatibility and without complete positivity; routes (i) and (iii) are unnecessary.  Theorem 80.9
is now conditional on (F1) only, given the 93.2 certificate.  Proposition 80.8 and Lemma 80.10 stay in force.

\subsection{What This Does Not Claim}

constructed for any \(\Cc\); (F1) is \(\mathsf R3\) and assumed.  The minimal-constant variant without (O), index
semicontinuity without a normal state, and non-vacuity for conformal-net inclusions are not established
(REVIEW.md:211--215).  Nothing identifies \(T\), characters, \(c\) or OPE data, gives \(\mathsf R5\), or shows lattice convergence to
